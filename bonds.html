What it is

Bonds are debt instruments issued by governments and companies that pay periodic interest and return the principal at maturity, making them a common building block for income and capital preservation.

  • Government securities. Backed by the sovereign, generally the highest credit quality available.
  • Corporate bonds. Issued by companies, rated by agencies such as CRISIL, ICRA and CARE.
  • Tax-efficient issues. Certain bonds carry specific tax treatment; rules can change, so we confirm current terms with you.
  • Laddering. Bonds of different maturities can be combined to manage reinvestment and liquidity needs.

Who this can suit

  • Investors seeking a predictable income stream.
  • Those wanting to balance the volatility of equity-linked holdings.
  • Retirees and conservative investors prioritising capital stability.
  • First-time investors, since some bonds start from as little as ₹10,000.

Risks and things to check

  • Credit risk: an issuer may delay or default on payments; ratings help assess this.
  • Interest-rate risk: bond prices can fall if interest rates rise before maturity.
  • Liquidity varies; some bonds are thinly traded if sold before maturity.
  • Tax treatment depends on the instrument and your holding period.
